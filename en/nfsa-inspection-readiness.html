PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR FOOD BUSINESSES

NFSA Inspection Readiness

Readiness is not a one-day exercise. It comes from a daily system that the team can operate and demonstrate through records, practices and corrective actions.

Prepared by Akram Sabry · Last reviewed: 12 November 2026

1. Start with a gap assessment

Break the facility into receiving, storage, processing, packing, hygiene, people and utilities. Record what is actually implemented and what still needs an action or objective evidence.

2. Organize documents and records

Use one control list for licenses and basic information, process flow, cleaning, pest control, maintenance, calibration, training, traceability and monitoring. A blank form is not evidence; records should be complete, dated and reviewed.

3. Walk the process on the floor

Follow product flow and check segregation, surfaces, equipment, temperatures, tools, protective clothing and packaging. Differences between written procedures and real practice are often the first gaps to close.

4. Test traceability and CAPA

Choose a batch and trace it back to the supplier and forward through the facility. Then test a nonconformity scenario: root cause, owner, action and effectiveness check.

5. Prepare the team

Every worker should know their role, what they monitor and what to do when there is a deviation. Short, task-based training is more useful than signatures alone.

Note: This is a general framework. Requirements vary by activity, scope and competent authority, so confirm the official requirements that apply to your facility.

Quick pre-inspection checklist

  • Core documents are current and accessible.
  • Records are complete and traceable to products or batches.
  • Internal findings have owners and due dates.
  • Traceability and CAPA have been tested.
  • Workers know their roles and escalation steps.
